Conference paper: Is Biomedical Engineering an Optional Extra or an Essential Part of Modern Medicine?
.

Abstract

.

Introduction

Medicine involves the use of clinical knowledge and scientific research to diagnose and treat diseases. However, with the increase of the population of people on earth and the more pressure that is existing on healthcare systems, the need for more technology has increased and is becoming vital in providing quality healthcare for patients, accuracy and efficiency for doctors.

This is where biomedical engineering comes in place as it allows us to use a mixture of principles in engineering with medical sciences to provide solutions for issues in healthcare such as artificial legs, radiography devices and artificial cells in the body. This begs the question: Is biomedical engineering an essential part of medicine in the modern day?

The relationship between biomedical engineering and Medicine?

Biomedical engineering is the unseen background of medicine. Doctors identify and provide treatments to sickness and illnesses. However, they use machines that they only know how to operate but not the inner structure of it. This explains the need for increased cooperation between engineers and medical personas. Dr Mann describes this relationship as important in order for more advanced healthcare technology to be achieved while Dr Pioletti describes it as a two-way exchange. Therefore, it is clear that biomedical engineering has become a contributor to medical progress.

Improving medical diagnosis

Medical imaging and diagnostic techniques such as MRI and ultrasound are examples of significant contributions by biomedical engineering. These techniques have helped doctors and clinical professionals to use safer methods to diagnose and treat patients rather than more invasive procedures. MRI scans involve taking use of the knowledge we have about magnetic fields and radiofrequency signals to produce detailed images of parts of the internal body which allows doctors to identify any abnormal conditions and change in the patients health. Also, Ultrasound is used to detect the location of the fetus in the womb aswell as cardiovascular tests. This shows that principles in engineering such as physics and electronics are used in high-end technologies which reflects how biomedical engineering helps improve information obtained by doctors and therefore allows them to make much more informed decisions about the disease. Dr Horeman has identified imaging technologies such as MRI and ultrasound as integral to modern medical practices. As a result, Biomedical engineering has become an essential part of diagnostic medicine. This also means that biomedical engineering can help in carrying out more complex treatment as it allows for the availability of more accurate devices.

Advancing medical treatment and patient care

Although the importance of biomedical engineering can be seen when it comes to diagnostic medicine, it has another role in treating diseases as it is allowing engineers to develop highly advanced technology such as pacemakers, prosthetic limbs, artificial joints and implantable medical devices. In pacemakers, the electrical aspect of biomedical engineering is used where a device is implanted that generates impulses that regulate the heartbeat for patients with cardiovascular diseases (CVD). Prosthetic limbs involve the application of material sciences and biomechanics to design devices that replace or support lost physical functions. This isnt only about providing a replacement for users of this device, it's about designing a machine that can resemble as closely as possible the original part of the body lost to help individuals regain aspects of their mobility and independence. This reflects the fact that engineering isnt only about making devices but also about creating technology that improves the quality of life of the patients which is seen by an article published by the World health organisation (WHO) which describes this technology as important tools in the prevention, diagnosis and treatment of illness.

Rehabilitation and improving quality of life

Rehabilitation is another part of medicine where biomedical engineering is becoming increasingly important in respect to patient care. It is allowing more advanced assistive technologies such as rehabilitation equipment to be developed allowing patients to manage their disabilities and recover physical functions such as supporting patients recovering from neurological conditions by assisting movement. Just like the technology used in diagnosing disease, the WHO has identified rehabilitation technology as important applications of medical equipment, mirroring the importance of medical technology across different stages of healthcare, not just in diagnosis and treatment. Therefore, biomedical engineering is helping improve the quality of life of patients by making them more independent in their everyday lives.

Challenges and limitations

Although it plays a significant part in modern day medicine, Biomedical engineering should be limited to what it interferes in. This means there should be a boundary preventing biomedical engineering from replacing medical knowledge and doctors' judgement of situations. Also, the new technology must be tested and peer reviewed many times to prevent any harm to potential users. Economically, the development of this technology requires massive investment and can be expensive to maintain and develop aswell as purchase resulting in an inequality for healthcare which contradicts what the NHS was created for which was equal health for all, the difference in equality was also highlighted by Dr Pioletti.

The expensive cost also means that this technology must be prioritised in the most essential cases and after careful consideration before becoming widespread which requires a collaboration between engineers, doctors, clinical scientists and healthcare organisations.

Conclusion

In conclusion, Biomedical engineering has become more than a supporting discipline in medicine. It has become part of it. The benefits it offers in diagnosing, treating and rehabilitating patients reflects this. Although these advantages offer a huge potential for this discipline to grow massively, the inequality, cost and access to this technology needs to be considered and improved for the impact it has on medicine to be more noticeable.
